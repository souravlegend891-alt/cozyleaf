What is glitch or Zalgo text?

Glitch text — often called "Zalgo text" or "cursed text" — is built by stacking Unicode combining marks (the small accents normally used above or below a single letter) on top of ordinary characters. Piling several of them onto every letter makes the text look like it's corrupting or breaking apart. Nothing is actually broken: it's still standard Unicode text, just layered very densely.

Why does it look different on different apps?

Every app renders combining marks slightly differently — some cap how many marks they'll stack per character, some clip or trim excess marks, and older systems may render them inconsistently. Heavy Zalgo text can look spectacular in one app and slightly tamer in another; the "Marks above / through / below" sliders let you dial the intensity down if a platform is cutting it off.

Is it safe to use in bios and usernames?

Yes — it's plain text under the hood, just with extra Unicode characters attached, so it copies and pastes like any other text. The length checker above shows how many characters your result actually uses against common platform limits, since heavily stacked Zalgo text can use up your character budget faster than it looks.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is glitch or Zalgo text? Glitch text, often called Zalgo or cursed text, is built by stacking Unicode combining marks on top of ordinary letters, making the text look like it's corrupting or breaking apart.

Will glitch text work on Instagram, Discord and TikTok? Yes — these are standard Unicode characters, so they display correctly anywhere that supports Unicode text. Very heavy Zalgo text may get visually trimmed on some platforms.

Why do some upside-down letters look different from the original? Unicode doesn't have a true rotated glyph for every letter, so the closest look-alike character is used. A few letters, like "l" or "o", stay the same.
