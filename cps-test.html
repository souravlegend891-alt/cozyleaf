What Is a CPS Test?

A CPS test measures how many times you can click a mouse button or tap a screen in a set amount of time. CPS stands for clicks per second — your total clicks divided by the length of the round. It started as a benchmark in the Minecraft PvP community, where faster clicking meant more hits landed per second, and has since grown into a general reflex and dexterity challenge people use to compare speed and technique.

How This Test Counts Clicks

This test uses pointer-down events, so every press registers the instant your finger or mouse button makes contact — not when it releases. That matters for rapid-fire techniques, since it means release speed never costs you a click. On a touchscreen, each finger is tracked as its own separate touch point, so tapping with two or three fingers at once genuinely counts every simultaneous tap. Many click tests only listen for a single generic "click" event, which can under-count true multi-touch tapping — this one doesn't.

Understanding Your Results

CPS is a rate, not a fixed speed — doubling the round length rarely doubles your total clicks, because sustained clicking is far harder to maintain than a short burst. This is why 60-second CPS scores are almost always lower than 1-second scores, and why scores from different durations aren't directly comparable to each other. The per-second breakdown chart on your results is useful here: it often shows a fast opening second or two, followed by a natural slowdown as fatigue sets in.
